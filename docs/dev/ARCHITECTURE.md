# How the class is built

This repository is one Eureka class folder (see Eureka's `docs/adding-a-class.md`). It also runs on its own: open
`index.html` from any static host, or straight from disk. Learning outcomes come first; Eureka's shared pieces (class
bar, companions, XP, settings, word bank) are used when the folder sits inside Eureka and are simply absent otherwise.
Two pages are Eureka's own pages inside Eureka, the front page and the reader. On its own the class shows its own
dashboard and its own reader in their place (`alone/`; see "Outside Eureka").

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Classic `<script>` tags and globals, no modules, no bundler, no network requests, no
external fonts or libraries: every page must work from `file://` and offline.

## The front page

`index.html` is a copy of ECON 101's front page, so that a later change to Eureka's version can be carried over with a
diff. It differs from ECON 101's where the class does: the title and eyebrow, no chart or market board in the hero, the
ten cards, the "Good to know" text, and the keys its page script reads. It loads Eureka's `settings.js`, `xp.js`,
`wardrobe.js`, `buddy.js`, `lookup.js` and `maison.css`; it does not load the class engine (`assets/core.js`), so it
reads `localStorage` itself. If `../shared/platform.js` is absent (the folder is used outside Eureka), a line at the top of
the head sends the visitor to `alone/index.html`, the class's own dashboard, which `page.js` draws.

The hero countdown and the Study Plan card come from Eureka (`shared/settings.js` and `shared/plan.js`). The exam date is
`orgo-exam-date`; the Study Plan is off until the student turns it on in Settings. `course.js` tells the plan where this
class keeps its progress:

| Plan block | Key | Shape it reads | Written by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `read` | `orgo-read-v1` | `{chapterId: {at, a, finished, done}}`, the 11 chapter ids in course order | the reader (`read/`) |
| `drill` | `orgo-drill-v1` | `{stats, hist, mark, days}` | `summary()` in `assets/core.js`, after every answer |
| `mock` | `orgo-mock-history-v1` | `[{at, right, total, pct, secs}]`, newest last, 50 at most | `mock/page.js` when a marked exam is saved (points earned and possible) |
| `review` | (none) | | the Review page lists the same questions |

There is no equations block, because the class has no equations trainer. Every card's status is computed by the page script
from the same stores: the drill record (Practice), `orgo-items-v1` (mechanisms drawn, and the Review List count), the mock
history, and `orgo-diag-v1` (Foundations). The number of mechanisms (45), lab tools (9), archive files (10) and word roots (94, which
`tools/check-roots.js` compares with `roots/roots.js`) are written in the page; change them with the content. A reading record from the first reader, `{sectionId: time, done, finished}`, counts
as a started chapter.

## The readings inside Eureka

Inside Eureka the readings are shown by the platform's reader, the page ECON 101 uses, so the class has the same look,
the same toolbar and the same settings: ErgoRead ("Aa"), the Word Bank, highlights and notes, badges with jewels,
Restart, and the reading levels once their texts are written. The class's own reader (`read/page.js`) is not shown
inside Eureka.

4. `drillRecord`: an answer at the exam level goes to the class's own store through

   answer, so a direct write to `orgo-drill-v1` would be lost. `quiet` means no event: the page sends its own.
5. `gemHTML`: the hint under a jewel names the Reading level button, and the jewels above Emerald, only when the

6. After a wrong answer, the question's own reason for the choice that was picked (`why` in `read/bank.js`), then

   The answer is A."), where the green choice above shows it.
7. `keep` and `tags` of a bank entry: the written order of the choices is kept where the explanation names them by
   their place, and they carry numbers where the question numbers them.
8. A passage without questions has no "Check yourself" heading.
9. `ReadChem.draw` after a chapter is drawn, before the notes are painted.
10. `ReadChem.draw` after a passage's questions are redrawn.
11. A flip card is not redrawn the moment it is turned. Every passage here has a one-card flip, and the redraw would

12. `route`: on the first route of a page that Back or Forward loads again, the scroll is left to the browser, because a

13. `go` and the `read:level` handler: when the reading levels arrive while the page is still scrolling to the current
    passage (a chapter just opened), the redraw lets that scroll go on; `scrollTo` would stop it partway.
14. `choicesNow`: an answer keeps the choices as they were worded when it was given. A choice corrected since (a drawing
    given its values) is matched to today's by what it draws, so the answered view shows today's choices with the right
    one marked; an answer that cannot be matched, or whose record disagrees with today's right answer, is offered again.

In `read/notes.js` the marked group makes the notes ignore what is not the passage's own words: the letters inside a
drawing, the line that opens a folded block, the words of a link, and the white space between two blocks. They are not
counted, not marked and not quoted (`ReadChem.text`, `ReadChem.skip`, `ReadChem.quote`). So a highlight keeps its place
whatever a drawing is made of, and a `<mark>` never lands inside an `<svg>`, where it would not be drawn.
`fit()`, also marked, keeps a highlight on its words when a passage's wording is corrected: a highlight whose words are
no longer at its place is moved to the nearest place where they are, and saved there.

### Carrying over a change from the platform's reader

When Eureka changes its reader, take the difference of ECON 101's files between the commit the copies were taken at
and the new one, and apply it to the copies:

```
cd <the Eureka checkout>
git diff 17b01be <new commit> -- econ101/read/index.html econ101/read/notes.js econ101/read/play.js > /tmp/reader.patch
cd <this repository>
git apply -p3 --directory=read --reject /tmp/reader.patch
```

`-p3 --directory=read` turns `econ101/read/<file>` into `read/<file>`. A hunk that touches a line this class changed is
left in a `.rej` file: apply it by hand beside the `CHEM 225:` comment on that line. Then write the new commit in place
of `17b01be` above (and the files' own commits in the table), try the reader in a browser inside Eureka (a chapter with drawings, a question right and wrong, a
highlight on a passage with a drawing, the end of a chapter), and run the build and the tests.

### The data the page reads

`node tools/build.js` writes three files from `content/read/*.json` and `content/items/*.json`. They are data only:
Eureka's own tools (`tools/check-levels.js`, `show-chapter.js`) load `read/chapters.js` and `read/bank.js` in node.

`read/chapters.js` sets `window.READ`, one chapter for each reading in course order:

| Field | From |
|---|---|
| `id` | the reading's id. It is also the key of its badge in Eureka (`shared/wardrobe.js`), of its progress, and of its level files |
| `title`, `topic` | `title`. Plain words: the reader also shows the title in the browser tab and in dialogs |
| `ch` | the lecture date ("Lecture 10/20"), or "Review" for a reading without one |
| `intro` | `from` and `goals`, as one paragraph of inline HTML |
| `sections` | one passage for each section, then "Homework from the lecture" (each prompt, with its worked steps and answer in a folded block) and "In short" (the summary). These two have no questions |

A passage is `{h, html, qs, play}`. `html` is the section's `html`, then each `micro` entry ("Closer look") and each
`macro` entry ("Bigger picture") as a `<details>` block, then a line that opens the pictures of the professor's pages
(`pages`), then the links described below. What happens to the questions of the section's `check` list:

| Kind | In the reader | Count today |
|---|---|---|
| `mcq` | asked under the passage: its id goes to `qs`, the question to `read/bank.js` | 76 |
| the section's `why` | a one-card `flip` titled "Before you move on" | 77 |
| `rank` | an `order` activity: the items in the right order, `how` from the item's `order` | 8 |
| `classify` | a `sort` activity with the item's bins | 2 |
| `multi` | a `sort` into two boxes named for what the stem asks (`BOXES` in `tools/build.js`) | 5 |
| `numeric` | a one-card `flip`: the question, then the answer with its unit and the solution's rule | 8 |
| `mech` | a sentence at the end of the passage with a link to that mechanism in the Mechanism Studio | 4 |
| `chair` | a sentence at the end of the passage with a link to that question type in Practice | 2 |

The activities of a passage come in this order: the why card, rankings, sortings, circle-all-that-apply, numbers.

`read/bank.js` sets `window.BANK = {title, storageKey: "orgo-drill-v1", q}`. An entry of `q` is
`{d, tag, q, ok, wrong, ex, why, keep, tags}`: `d` and `tag` the question type, `q` the stem, `ok` the right choice,
`wrong` the others, `ex` the reason for the right choice followed by the solution's answer line (and the item's `note`),
`why` a map from each wrong choice to the reason it is wrong. `keep` (only where the item keeps its written order) is the
place of the right choice in that order. `tags` (only where the question numbers its choices) is `"12345"`.

`read/figures.js` calls `ORGO.reg` with the data of every structure those two files ask for, and with the mechanisms
shown inside a reading, so that a passage is drawn at once with nothing to fetch.

`{{formulas}}` and `[[figures]]` stay as written in all these strings, in the answers the reader saves (a choice is
known by its exact text), and in the reading-level files. `read/chem.js` draws them in the page.

### Positions do not move

The reader files a student's place, the answers to activities, the notes and the reading-level texts by position:
passage 3 of a chapter, activity 2 of a passage. `read/positions.json` records where every passage, question and
activity sits. The build stops when a position that is recorded would change, and says which. So a new section goes
after the sections that are there, a new question after the questions of its section, and so on. Two cases move
something that is there and need a decision: a new section in a reading (the homework and summary passages come after
it), and a new activity of a kind that comes earlier in the order above. `node tools/build.js --accept-moves` writes
anyway; students then lose their place in that reading, and notes and level texts of the moved passages belong to
the wrong passage until they are moved too.

After writing, the build reads the three files back and checks them as the page will read them: every question a
passage names is in the bank, every figure has its data and is of a kind the page can draw, no question has more than
five choices or two choices that read the same, and `positions.json` agrees with `chapters.js`.
`node tools/build.js --check` runs the same checks on the files that are there and says when the content has changed
since they were written.

### `read/chem.js`

```
ReadChem.draw(root)     draw every {{formula}} and [[figure]] in the text under root. What is drawn stays, so it is safe to call again
ReadChem.text(range)    the passage's own words in a range     }
ReadChem.quote(range)   the same words as the notebook shows   }  used by read/notes.js
ReadChem.skip(node)     is this text node not the passage's?   }
```

It works on the text the reader has already put in the page (text nodes, never `innerHTML`), with the class's own
handlers (`ORGO.tokens`, `ORGO.formula`). A figure alone on its line is a figure of its own, a figure inside a sentence
is drawn small: the rule of `ORGO.html`, read from the page. Every drawing gets `data-nolookup` on its `svg` and every
formula is one element with `data-lk`, which is what Eureka's dictionary looks for.

The page calls `draw` when it has drawn a chapter and when it has redrawn a passage's questions. `chem.js` also draws
before the notes paint (it wraps `ReadNotes.apply` and `ReadNotes.repaint`, which `shared/readlevels.js` calls after it
swaps a passage to another level), and it watches the reader's view and draws whatever else arrives there, such as an
activity that `read/play.js` redraws. Drawing always comes before the notes, because a note is filed by the place of
its words.

It does two more things. Before the reader reads `orgo-read-v1`, a chapter saved by the class's own reader
(`{sectionId: time, done, finished}`) is turned into the reader's shape, once: `at` is the number of sections read,
`done` and `finished` are kept, and there are no answers. And a link "Original notes: 10/20 page 1" opens the picture
in the reader's own viewer.

`read/figures.css` holds what the reader's look does not have: the drawings (the rules of `assets/mol.css` and
`assets/chair.css`, with the colours they ask for given from the reader's own, so they follow Light, Dark and Stars),
the folded blocks and tables inside a passage, drawings in questions and choices, and flip cards tall enough for a
whole question. `assets/orgo.css`, `quiz.css` and `study.css` are not loaded on that page: their `.q`, `.opt`, `.card`
and `.btn` rules would collide with the reader's.

The labels inside a drawing keep the weight, the spacing and the typeface they are drawn with on the class's own
pages. The words around a drawing would hand theirs down (a bold card, the tighter line of a question, the word
spacing of a passage), so the wrapper of every figure sets them back. Eureka's reading preferences (wider spacing,
the easier-to-read typeface) are set on everything inside a passage and a choice, so the last rules of the file give
the labels their own back.

### Reading levels

The level and density dials are Eureka's (`shared/readlevels.js`). They appear when `read/levels.js` exists. Until the
level texts are written there is no such file: the page asks for it once, the request fails, and the readings are at
the exam level. The texts are written and checked with Eureka's tools (`tools/check-levels.js` and the others, run
from a Eureka checkout that holds this class as `chem225/`): `read/levels/<chapter>.js` by passage position,
`<chapter>.q.js` by question id, `<chapter>.p.js` by activity position. They may carry `{{formulas}}` and
`[[figures]]`, which are drawn like the rest. The build reads the level files when they exist: it checks their figures
as it checks content, and puts the structures they need into `read/figures.js`. So run `node tools/build.js` after
level texts are written or changed, and commit `read/figures.js` with them.

A level text is words only: Eureka's checker refuses links and folded blocks. At another level the platform hides the
passage as written and shows the level text beside it, so `read/chem.js` copies what hangs under the passage to the
end of its level text: the line with the links to the professor's pages, and the link to a mechanism or a chair to
draw. The closer looks and bigger pictures are not copied. A level text is written from the whole passage, those
included. Every chapter also has its two last passages to write at each level, the homework and "In short".

## Outside Eureka

The front page, the reader and Word Roots are made of Eureka's shared files and cannot work without them. Outside Eureka
(`../shared/platform.js` is absent) each of them sends the browser to the class's own page for the same job:
`index.html` to `alone/index.html` (the dashboard, `page.js`), `read/index.html` to `alone/read/index.html` (the
class's own reader, `read/page.js`) and `roots/index.html` to `alone/roots/index.html`, with the same query and hash.
Inside Eureka nothing links to `alone/`, and a visit there is sent back to Eureka's page. `tools/shells.js` writes the
three `alone/` pages and the nine other shells (`alone/roots/index.html` from `roots/index.html` itself, in the class's
look); it does not write `index.html`, `read/index.html` or `roots/index.html`, which are kept by hand.

The class's own reader keeps `orgo-read-v1` in its own shape. It also reads the reader's shape (the first `at`
sections count as read), so it never stops on a save made inside Eureka.

### Scheduling and mastery: `ORGO.learn` (in `core.js`)

One record per item, in `orgo-items-v1`:

```
{ "<item id>": { n: attempts, ok: correct, streak, box: 0..5, due: epoch ms, last: epoch ms, conf: 0..2, flag: bool,
                 first: epoch ms, hist: [[epoch ms, score 0..1, conf, seconds], … last 12] } }
```

```
ORGO.learn.record(id, {score, conf, secs, quiet})   score 0..1 (1 right, 0 wrong, in between for rubric-graded work).
                                             quiet: the record, the schedule and the summary change, and no event goes
                                             to Eureka (for a caller that sends its own, like the reader)
ORGO.learn.state(id)                         the record, or null
ORGO.learn.flag(id, on)
ORGO.learn.due(now)                          ids whose review is due, most overdue first
ORGO.learn.pick({types, n, mode})            ids for a session. mode "review" (due only), "learn" (new, one type),
                                             "mixed" (due first, then weakest types, interleaved so that no two
                                             neighbours share a type)
ORGO.learn.mastery(typeId)                   {level 0..4, label, seen, total, recent, delayed}
ORGO.learn.examDate() / setExamDate(iso)     stored in orgo-prefs-v1
ORGO.learn.daysLeft()
ORGO.learn.plan()                            today's session: [{step, title, detail, href, count, done}]
```

Rules, all from the research in `docs/learning-science.md`:

- **Successive relearning.** A missed item comes back in the same session after at least three other items, until it is
  answered right once. Then it returns on later days.
- **Spacing sized to the exam.** After a correct answer the gap grows through 1, 2, 4, 7, 14 days, but is cut so that
  at least two more reviews fit before the exam date when one is set.
- **Mastery is delayed, not same-day.** Levels: 0 new, 1 seen, 2 practising, 3 solid (three right in a row on different
  questions of the type), 4 retained (right again after a day or more). The number shown first is delayed accuracy.
- **Mixed by default.** Once a type has been met, its questions arrive mixed with others, because choosing the approach
  is the skill the exam tests.
- **Confidence before feedback** on written and mechanism work (guess, unsure, sure), so that confident errors are
  pointed out.

### `mol.js`: structures, reactions, curved arrows

Drawings come from `ORGO.data.mols[key]`, made by `tools/chem.py`:
`{w, h, p: [[bond or -1, path d, kind 0 line | 1 hash | 2 wedge]], t: [[atom, x, y, size, text]], a: [[x, y, element, charge, lone pairs, H count, map]], b: [[i, j, order]]}`.

```
ORGO.mol.svg(key, {scale, inline, label})        -> <svg> of one drawing (ink follows the theme: currentColor)
ORGO.mol.scheme(tokenArg, kind)                   -> element for [[rxn:…]] / eq / eqr / eql / nr
ORGO.mol.stage(el, {species, arrows, mode, onChange})   a mechanism step
      mode "show"   draw the structures with the given arrows
      mode "play"   draw, then animate the arrows one at a time
      mode "input"  the student draws arrows: press a source (an atom means its lone pair, or a bond), then a target
      returns {arrows(), set(arrows), clear(), undo(), check(expected) -> {ok, score, notes: [{arrow, kind, text}]}, destroy()}
ORGO.mol.apply(drawing, arrows)                   -> {problems: [text]}   the same valence rules as tools/chem.py
ORGO.mol.same(a, b)                               arrows equal as electron movements, whatever way they were clicked
```

Atoms are addressed by **map number**. Arrow shape: a curved line from the source (a lone pair is drawn as two dots at
the start) to the target, with a full head. Hit targets are at least 28 px, keyboard reachable (Tab through atoms and
bonds, Enter to pick), and every target has an accessible name ("oxygen 1, lone pair", "bond carbon 4 to bromine 5").

Feedback on a wrong set of arrows names the kind of error, in this order: an arrow that starts where there are no
electrons; an arrow drawn backwards (its reverse is correct); a missing arrow that leaves an atom with too many bonds;
an extra arrow; otherwise the first hint.

### `mech.js`: the mechanism trainer

```
ORGO.mech.mount(el, mechId, {mode})    mode "watch" (step through, arrows animate, read say/why)
                                           "complete" (some arrows drawn, the student adds the missing ones)
                                           "solo" (the student draws every arrow of every step)
                                           "paper" (draw it on paper first, then reveal step by step and self-check)
ORGO.mech.card(mechId)                 a compact static view: overall reaction and all steps with arrows (for solutions)
```

A step is checked before the next is shown. A hint is offered only after an attempt; a step finished on the last hint
does not count toward mastery. Each finished mechanism reports `ORGO.learn.record("mech:" + id, …)`.

### `chair.js`: cyclohexanes

Substituents are `{c: 1..6, face: "u" | "d", g: "CH3"}`. In form A the axial bond of carbons 1, 3, 5 points up and of
2, 4, 6 points down; form B is the ring-flipped chair.

```
ORGO.chair.parse("1u:CH3,2u:CH3")       -> subs
ORGO.chair.pos(sub, form)                -> "ax" | "eq"
ORGO.chair.analyze(subs)                 -> {cisTrans: "cis" | "trans" | null, forms: {A: {axial: [...], cost}, B: {...}},
                                             stable: "A" | "B" | "equal", relation: "same" | "enantiomers" | "diastereomers",
                                             chiral: bool, steps: [text]}     (steps explain the answer in words)
ORGO.chair.e2(subs, lgCarbon)            -> {reactiveForm, antiH: [carbons], products: [...], steps: [text]}
ORGO.chair.svg(subs, form, {num, H, highlight})   the chair drawing
ORGO.chair.ring(subs, {num})                       the flat wedge/dash drawing
ORGO.chair.lab(el, {subs})               interactive: add groups, flip the ring (animated), see energies and the verdict
ORGO.chair.ask(el, item, onDone)         a chair question (cis-trans, stable, flip, relationship, e2)
ORGO.chair.make(kind, seed)              a fresh random chair question of a kind
ORGO.chair.A                              the A-value table {group: {a: kcal/mol, src: "lecture" | "literature"}}
```

`relation` is decided by symmetry, not by rule of thumb: bring the flipped chair into the frame of the first by a 60°
turn about the ring axis, then test the six rotations of the chair (same) and its six reflections and
rotation-reflections (enantiomers); anything else is diastereomers.

### `quiz.js`: questions

```
ORGO.quiz.mount(el, item, {onDone, review})     draw one item of any kind and handle the whole exchange:
                                                 answer -> (confidence) -> right/wrong with the reason for the chosen
                                                 answer -> worked solution, step by step -> record
ORGO.quiz.session(el, ids, {title, onEnd})      a run of items with a progress strip; misses come back; ends with a summary
ORGO.quiz.worked(el, item)                      a worked example: steps revealed one at a time, with its "ask" prompts
ORGO.quiz.solution(item)                        the solution block as an Element
```

Written items: the stem, a timer suggestion and "I have written my answer" come first; then the model answer beside the
rubric, each rubric line ticked full, half or none; the score is points earned over points possible.

## Storage keys

All under the prefix `orgo-`: `orgo-items-v1` (every answer), `orgo-prefs-v1` (exam date, daily minutes), `orgo-read-v1`
(the readings: see below), `orgo-notes-v1` (highlights and notes made in Eureka's reader), `orgo-mock-v1` (mock exam

`orgo-read-v1` has two shapes. Inside Eureka the reader keeps `{chapterId: {at, a, w, wOld, finished, done, gem}}`:

`w` the finished activities by position, `done` the number of times the chapter was finished, `gem` the jewel on its
badge. Outside Eureka the class's own reader keeps `{chapterId: {sectionId: time, done, finished}}`, and
`orgo-read-why-v1` for its why cards. Each reader reads the other's shape (see `read/chem.js` and `read/page.js`).

## Looks

`assets/orgo.css` carries the whole look on its own, in Eureka's style: system font, white and light gray surfaces,
blue for anything that can be pressed, rounded cards, pill buttons, three appearances (light, dark, night). Inside Eureka,
`shared/maison.css` loads after it and the two agree. Right and wrong are never shown by colour alone (a tick or a cross
and a word go with it). Everything works with the keyboard, at 200% text size, and with motion reduced.

Structures are drawn in the page's ink colour. Colour is used for meaning only, always with a second cue:
electron-movement arrows (accent), axial and equatorial bonds (two hues plus the letters a and e), the nucleophile and
the leaving group (labels).
